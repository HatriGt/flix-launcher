Choosing the best Android TV launcher

A launcher is the app you see every time you press Home. The right one makes your TV feel fast and calm; the wrong one is another place for adverts. Here's what to check before you install one.

Five things that matter

1. Does the Home button actually open it?

On Google TV (Chromecast with Google TV, recent Sony, TCL and Hisense sets), the Home button ignores your default launcher. A launcher has to handle the Home key itself, usually through an accessibility service. If it doesn't, you'll keep landing back on the stock screen.

2. Ads and tracking

Some launchers replace Google's adverts with their own. Check whether it's open source and whether it has analytics.

3. Speed and memory

Most Android TVs have 1.5 to 2 GB of memory. A launcher that holds every poster in memory will push your streaming app out of it. Look at the APK size and how it loads artwork.

4. Does it show what you watch?

An app grid is fine, but the point of a TV is watching. A launcher that shows the episode you were halfway through saves several clicks every evening.

5. Remote-friendly settings

You'll be setting it up with a D-pad. Searchable settings and adding things from your phone make a real difference.

Questions

What is the best ad-free Android TV launcher?

Flix Launcher, Projectivy Launcher and FLauncher are all free of adverts. Flix focuses on watching (Continue watching, film rows, trailers); FLauncher is a minimal app grid; Projectivy is highly customisable.

Will a custom launcher work on Google TV?

Yes, but Google TV sends the Home button to its own launcher. Pick one that handles the Home key, usually through an accessibility service, as Flix does.

Does a launcher slow down my TV?

It can. Heavy launchers keep a lot of artwork in memory. Flix uses about 54 MB on a 2 GB TV.
